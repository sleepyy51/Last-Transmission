Clear errors

1. Apollo 11 Lunar Module “Eagle”

Claim: The ascent stage (“sister”) would return home. The text also implies she was dropped in orbit and crashed months later.
Correction: The ascent stage never returned to Earth. Only the crew did, in the command module Columbia. The ascent stage was jettisoned in lunar orbit, and NASA’s catalog lists its impact site as unknown. The “crashed months later” claim is unconfirmed, since the stage was never tracked after being jettisoned.

2. Lunar Roving Vehicle (Apollo 15)

Claim: The astronauts rode the rover for “almost 18 and a half hours.”
Correction: 18 h 35 min is the total time of the three moonwalks (EVAs). Actual driving time was just over three hours (about 3 h 02 min). The distances in the story are correct.

3. ALSEP (Apollo 12)

Claim: The ALSEP was assembled about 180 meters from the Surveyor 3 robot.
Correction: The 180 m figure is the distance between the Apollo 12 lunar module and Surveyor 3. The ALSEP was deployed 600 to 700 feet (about 180–215 m) from the lunar module. NASA does not give an ALSEP-to-Surveyor distance.

4. Surveyor 3

Claim: It sent 6,326 photos.
Correction: NASA’s own sources disagree. NASA’s current mission page says 6,326 TV pictures, while a 1969 NASA technical report says 6,315 pictures taken between April 20 and May 3, 1967. Either is defensible, but they conflict.

Imprecisions worth a narrator note

5. Pioneer 10

Claim: It crossed Neptune’s orbit, “the last planet they knew.”
Correction: In 1983 Neptune was the outermost planet because Pluto’s orbit had carried it inside Neptune’s path. NASA describes Neptune as “then the outermost planet.” Pluto was known, just temporarily closer to the Sun.

6. Opportunity’s letter to Spirit

Claim: The “last message” (“it’s getting dark and my battery is low”).
Correction: That line is a journalistic paraphrase of the final data, not a literal transmission.
Claim: “90 days.”
Correction: The design life was 90 sols (Martian days), which are slightly longer than Earth days.
Claim: Landing dates of Jan 3 and Jan 24, 2004.
Correction: These match U.S. Pacific time. In UTC they are Jan 4 and Jan 25.
Claim: The Perseverance record in October of “this year.”
Correction: This is accurate: Perseverance passed Opportunity’s 45.16 km mark on October 11, 2026. However, Opportunity “writing” after 2018 and knowing this is a narrative device, not a fact.
